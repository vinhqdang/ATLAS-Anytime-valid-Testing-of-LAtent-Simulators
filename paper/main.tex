\title{ATLAS: Anytime-Valid Monitoring and Certification of Neural World Models}

\author[buv]{Quang-Vinh Dang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author. ORCID: 0000-0002-3877-8024}
\affiliation[buv]{organization={British University Vietnam}, city={Hung Yen}, country={Vietnam}}

\begin{abstract}
A world model supports planning only while its imagined rollouts stay close to what the environment would do, and that closeness depends on the rollout horizon and changes during deployment. Offline scores are computed once and follow neither effect. We study sequential monitoring and certification of deployed neural world models with error guarantees that hold uniformly over time and need no assumption on how the planner generates the data. ATLAS runs a betting test martingale for each horizon against the null that the horizon is $\varepsilon$-faithful, giving a \emph{revocation frontier} that controls false alarms, and a confidence sequence on the faithfulness gap, giving a \emph{certified horizon} that controls the opposite, safety-relevant error. We prove time-uniform validity, a correct confidence sequence for the running average of conditional means with a per-horizon total-variation certificate, and a finite-sample detection-delay bound. On a simulator with known truth, a first-order delay law predicts measured delay within $20\%$ and the certified bound is never violated. On a closed-loop stream steered by a model-based planner, CUSUM and a conformal martingale on raw prediction error alarm in every run while ATLAS stays at or below $0.013$. Gating a planner built on a neural dynamics model with ATLAS lowers the mean post-fault cost over three faults from $34.4$ to $8.2$--$13.9$ with no cost in fault-free operation, though it reacts more slowly than CUSUM. On real video, a frozen-encoder model reaches zero false alarms and $0.9$--$1.0$ detection at a tolerance margin of one standard deviation, while a small convolutional autoencoder does not. Two negative results: pooling overlapping horizon chains does not recover the data they discard, and a tolerance calibrated on pooled data voids the conditional guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Anytime-valid monitoring of deployed neural world models, resolved by horizon
\item Separate guarantees for false alarms (frontier) and for safety (certified horizon)
\item Valid on policy-steered streams; raw-error monitors inflate false alarms there
\item A drift gate cuts planning cost under actuator faults for a neural dynamics model
\item Tolerance calibration decides real-video false alarms; negative results reported
\end{highlights}
