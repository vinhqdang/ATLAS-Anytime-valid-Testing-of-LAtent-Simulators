ADDITIONAL SUBMISSION INFORMATION (unchanged from the original submission)

Importance and fit to scope. World models -- learned, action-conditioned simulators used for
video prediction, autonomous driving, and model-based decision making -- are now central to
pattern recognition research, yet the field still evaluates them with a single offline number
(Frechet Video Distance, reconstruction error, return gap) computed once before deployment. This
manuscript shows that such offline scores are structurally blind to how a world model's
faithfulness actually behaves: it decays with rollout horizon and drifts over deployment time,
especially once the model is embedded in a closed decision loop that endogenously shapes its own
test data. ATLAS instead gives a deployed world model a running, anytime-valid statistical
certificate -- a trust horizon that a downstream planner can use directly to clip its imagination
depth -- validated on a controllable synthetic simulator, four real video benchmarks (Moving
MNIST, KTH Actions, KITTI driving, and UZH-FPV drone flight), and learned neural world models.

Comparison to the state of the art. The manuscript positions ATLAS against, and empirically
compares it with, recent representative work, including Shin, Ramdas, and Rinaldo (2024,
"E-detectors"), the closest anytime-valid change-detection framework, compared against directly
in Table 2; Gibbs and Candes (2021) and its 2025-2026 online-conformal descendants (Leong, 2026;
Opoku and Banahene, 2026), representative of the conformal-prediction baselines in Table 2;
Assran et al. (2025, "V-JEPA 2") and NVIDIA et al. (2025, "Cosmos"), state-of-the-art world models
exemplifying the class of representation ATLAS certifies; and, newly added in this revision,
Msaddi and Kumbasar (2025) and Su et al. (2024) from Pattern Recognition itself. We are not aware
of prior work that gives a horizon-resolved, anytime-valid faithfulness certificate for world
models under policy-endogenous data.
