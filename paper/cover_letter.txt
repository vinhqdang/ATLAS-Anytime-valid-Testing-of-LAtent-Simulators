Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, "ATLAS: Anytime-valid Testing of Latent Simulators —
Sequential Certification of World-Model Faithfulness via E-Process Betting," for consideration
as a research article in Pattern Recognition. This manuscript is original, has not been
published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. I am the sole author and
declare no competing interests.

Importance and fit to scope. World models — learned, action-conditioned simulators used for
video prediction, autonomous driving, and model-based decision making — are now central to
pattern recognition research, yet the field still evaluates them with a single offline number
(Frechet Video Distance, reconstruction error, return gap) computed once before deployment.
This manuscript shows that such offline scores are structurally blind to how a world model's
faithfulness actually behaves: it decays with rollout horizon and drifts over deployment time,
especially once the model is embedded in a closed decision loop that endogenously shapes its
own test data. We introduce ATLAS, which instead gives a deployed world model a running,
anytime-valid statistical certificate — a trust horizon that a downstream planner can use
directly to clip its imagination depth. The method is representation-agnostic (pixels, latent
codes, or embeddings), requires no exchangeability assumption, and comes with three proved
guarantees (uniform validity, an anytime-valid simulation lemma, and an order-optimal detection-
delay bound), validated on a controllable synthetic simulator, four real video benchmarks
(Moving MNIST, KTH Actions, KITTI driving, and UZH-FPV drone flight), and learned neural world
models. This combination of sequential statistical testing, representation learning, and
pattern-recognition benchmarks (video, driving, drone imagery) sits squarely within the scope of
Pattern Recognition.

Comparison to the state of the art. The manuscript positions ATLAS against, and empirically
compares it with, recent representative work, including:
  1. Shin, Ramdas, and Rinaldo (2024), "E-detectors: A nonparametric framework for sequential
     change detection" — the closest anytime-valid change-detection framework, which we
     specialize and compare against directly (Table 4).
  2. Gibbs and Candes (2021), "Adaptive conformal inference under distribution shift," and its
     2026 online-conformal descendants (Leong, 2026, "Online shift detection and conformal
     adaptation for deployed safety classifiers"; Opoku and Banahene, 2026, "Drift-aware
     spectral conformal prediction for non-exchangeable streaming data") — representative of
     the conformal-prediction line we compare against as an anytime-valid baseline and discuss
     as lacking a horizon-resolved, endogeneity-robust guarantee.
  3. Assran et al. (2025), "V-JEPA 2: Self-supervised video models enable understanding,
     prediction and planning" — a state-of-the-art action-conditioned video world model that
     exemplifies the class of representation ATLAS is designed to certify.
  4. NVIDIA et al. (2025), "Cosmos world foundation model platform for physical AI" — a
     state-of-the-art large-scale physical-AI world-model platform illustrating the deployment
     setting (offline-evaluated, production world models) that motivates this work.
We are not aware of prior work that gives a horizon-resolved, anytime-valid faithfulness
certificate for world models under policy-endogenous data; Section 1.4 (Related work) and
Section 5.4 (Comparison with competitor monitors) detail this positioning and the empirical
gap quantitatively.
