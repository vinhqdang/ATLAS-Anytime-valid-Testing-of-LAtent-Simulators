\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A model-based agent imagines several futures, dozens of steps deep, at every decision, and acts on
the one that looks best. That is sensible while the world model matches the environment. Then a
joint wears, friction changes, a payload shifts, or the agent's own improving policy carries it into
states the model never saw. The imagined futures stay confident, nothing in the monitoring stack
fires, because the only number anyone computed was an offline score on held-out data before
deployment, and the failure is found by its consequences.

Two properties make the faithfulness of a world model awkward to certify. It \emph{decays with the
horizon}: a rollout applies the model recursively, so a small one-step error compounds, and a model
that is nearly perfect one step ahead can be useless twenty steps ahead. Faithfulness is therefore a
profile over horizons rather than a scalar. It also \emph{drifts over deployment time}, through
non-stationarity, the sim-to-real gap, and the feedback loop of model-based reinforcement learning,
where the policy generating the data is optimized through the model under test. The data stream
is then \emph{endogenous}: it depends on the object being audited.

Current practice scores world models with Fr\'echet Video Distance \citep{unterthiner2018fvd},
reconstruction error, or return gaps, all computed once on a fixed set. Conformal prediction
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic} and its online variants \citep{gibbs2021adaptive,gibbs2024conformal}
are the usual tools for distribution-free uncertainty, but they target marginal coverage of a single
prediction. Classical change detectors (CUSUM, Page--Hinkley, windowed tests) control an average run
length, so their false-alarm probability grows with monitoring time. What is wanted instead is a
running statement, ``at round $t$, rollouts of this model are trustworthy up to horizon $h$ at
tolerance $\eps$,'' whose error probability is controlled uniformly over all rounds, resolved by
horizon, indifferent to whether the model predicts pixels or embeddings, and sound when the planner
steers the data.

\paragraph{Approach.}
We build this from the testing-by-betting machinery of anytime-valid inference
\citep{shafer2021testing,ramdas2023savi,waudbysmith2024betting}. At each round the model emits, for
each horizon, an action-conditioned predictive distribution; reality later reveals the outcome. A
strictly proper score \citep{gneiting2007strictly} turns the pair into a bounded statistic. For each
horizon a test martingale bets against the null that the horizon is $\eps$-faithful, which is a
\emph{conditional-mean} null, one constraint given the whole past; that is why no exchangeability
or independence is needed and why the construction survives policy endogeneity. The same
betting machinery, run as a confidence sequence, bounds the faithfulness gap from above. These two
outputs answer different questions and control different errors:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item the \emph{revocation frontier} $\hstar(t)$, the largest horizon whose e-processes have not
yet crossed $1/\alpha$, is a monitor: a faithful horizon is revoked with probability at most
$\alpha$ over all time (an error of availability);
\item the \emph{certified horizon} $\hcert_\tau(t)$, the largest horizon whose confidence-sequence
bound on total variation is below a budget $\tau$, is a certificate: with probability at least
$1-\alpha$, every horizon the planner is allowed to use has window-average discrepancy at most $\tau$ (an error of safety).
\end{itemize}

A planner that clips its imagination depth to $\min(\hstar,\hcert_\tau)$ inherits both guarantees, jointly with probability at least $1-2\alpha$ (the certificate is an average over a window of logged rounds, see Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}).
The two must not be conflated: a bound on false revocations says nothing about trusting an
unfaithful horizon.

\paragraph{What is and is not new.}
Betting e-processes and confidence sequences are not new, and neither is sequential forecast
evaluation with e-values (Section~\ref{sec:related}). We do not claim a new inference principle. The
contributions are the problem formalization for deployed world models, the analysis of what breaks
when the target is multi-step, and a body of evidence about when the construction works and when it
does not:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Formalization and multi-step validity} (Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:method}).
A horizon-indexed, conditional-mean faithfulness null with a tolerance, a statistic whose
conditional mean is the object of interest, and an analysis of why consecutive $h$-step residuals
cannot be bet on directly. We validate on non-overlapping chains and show, as a negative result, that the
obvious attempt to recover the discarded data by pooling offset chains does not help.
\item \textbf{Two dual guarantees} (Section~\ref{sec:theory}): time-uniform false-revocation control;
a confidence sequence for the running average of \emph{conditional} means (the constant-mean
construction does not certify it); a windowed variant; and a per-horizon total-variation
certificate with a simulation-lemma-style value-gap consequence.
\item \textbf{A finite-sample delay bound} replacing the asymptotic statement, with a
horizon-resolved growth rate that predicts measured delay across horizons.
\item \textbf{Evidence} (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}): ground-truth validation including the delay
law and the certificate; a closed-loop study where the planner steers the stream; a replicated
real-video study with competitor monitors on identical streams; and ablations that include the
cases where design choices fail.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\emph{Anytime-valid inference and betting.} Ville's inequality \citep{ville1939}, game-theoretic
probability and e-values \citep{shafer2019game,vovk2021evalues,ramdas2023savi}, safe testing
\citep{gruenwald2024safe}, and betting confidence sequences \citep{waudbysmith2024betting,
howard2021timeuniform} supply our tools. Betting has been applied to two-sample testing
\citep{shekhar2023nonparametric}, to auditing fairness of a deployed classifier
\citep{chugg2023auditing}, and to tracking the risk of a deployed model under shift
\citep{podkopaev2022tracking}.

\emph{Sequential forecast evaluation.} The closest prior work evaluates probabilistic forecasts
sequentially with e-values: calibration tests for probability forecasts \citep{arnold2023sequentially},
comparison of forecasters \citep{henzi2022valid,choe2024comparing}. These establish that
time-uniform inference on forecast scores is possible, and one of them has direct bearing on our
setting: a correction to \citet{henzi2022valid} withdraws a supermartingale property for horizons
above one, the same overlap phenomenon that we analyze in Section~\ref{sec:overlap}. They address
calibration or relative performance of a forecaster at a fixed lead time. We instead test an
absolute tolerance null at every horizon of an action-conditioned simulator, certify the gap from
above, and study streams in which the actions depend on the model.

\emph{Conformal prediction and change detection.} Conformal test martingales
\citep{vovk2003testing} monitor exchangeability, and online conformal methods
\citep{gibbs2021adaptive,gibbs2024conformal,tibshirani2019covariate} relax it for coverage.
Change detectors based on cumulative sums, Page--Hinkley, windowing, and Bayesian run-length
posteriors \citep{page1954cusum,bifet2007adwin,raab2020kswin,adams2007bocpd} are tuned to an average
run length, and the e-detector framework \citep{shin2024edetectors} makes the false-alarm guarantee
nonparametric. We compare against all of these in Section~\ref{sec:exp-baselines}.

\emph{World models and the simulation lemma.} World models range from latent-imagination agents
\citep{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2023dreamerv3} and joint-embedding predictive architectures
\citep{assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa,assran2025vjepa2} to interactive and physical-AI simulators
\citep{bruce2024genie,nvidia2025cosmos}. Their evaluation is offline. The simulation lemma
\citep{kearns2002near,janner2019mbpo,lobel2024tightness} turns one-step model error into a value
gap, with a known one-step error. We replace the known error by a time-uniform certified bound.
